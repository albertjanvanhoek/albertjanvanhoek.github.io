Albert Jan van Hoek

I study how things spread, persist and learn: in epidemics, in societies, and in minds.

Infectious disease modeller in the Netherlands. Working on a theory of emergence and persistence. Making music as Emergence.

Science

I am a senior infectious disease modeller and principal investigator at RIVM, the Dutch National Institute for Public Health and the Environment. I lead Infectieradar, the Dutch participatory surveillance cohort and part of the European Influenzanet network, in which people report their own symptoms every week.

My work has moved across epidemiology and health economics, the carriage dynamics of pneumococcus and meningococcus, natural language processing, simulation-based inference for epidemics in structured populations, and, more recently, agentic AI.

What connects it all is a question about populations: how individual contacts and choices add up to something none of the individuals planned.

Ideas

Evolution by Emergence is a long-running project to describe persistence in a way that does not depend on the substrate. Cells, organisations, societies and artificial systems all face the same problem: to keep existing in a changing world, they have to keep learning, and they cannot do that alone. The working subtitle of the book is How Networks Build, Retain and Transform Their Capacities.

Keep alive what keeps you alive, and improve it for the next generation.

It starts from a thought experiment I call the Room. Four people from four traditions sit together, each equally certain, each holding a view the others cannot share. They cannot all be right. So inner conviction is not a reliable sign of truth, and correction by others is not optional: it is how any mind, human or artificial, stays connected to the world.

From there the work goes into world models and their integrity, distributed regulation instead of capture, and what we owe each other once we accept that we depend on one another. In short: disco ergo sum, I learn, therefore I am.
